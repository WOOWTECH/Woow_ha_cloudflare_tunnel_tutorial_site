Chapter 5

One-time Cloudflare authorization

The first time you start in locally managed mode, Setup shows a sensitive Cloudflare authorization link. You will learn how to check the account and the zone in your own environment, how to run the acceptance check afterwards, and why you must never hand the link, the certificate or the screen to anyone else.

This is not an ordinary sign-in link

A locally managed tunnel has to run cloudflared tunnel login first. That command starts a browser authorization flow that has you sign in to Cloudflare and pick the domain zone to authorize; on success it downloads the account certificate cert.pem, and only then can the add-on create the tunnel and the DNS route. Setup in the Web GUI only takes the link that used to be buried in Logs and shows it as a button you can click.

This flow is closer to pairing a new key with your own front-door lock than to reading a public web page. The authorization URL, the sign-in session and the certificate it produces can all affect your Cloudflare resources. Do not forward it to a friend to "press it for you", do not put it in a public issue, and do not run it for real in an environment that is not supposed to change just to get a screenshot.

Capture environment disclosure: the capture environment for this site never clicked the authorization link, never signed in to Cloudflare, never picked a zone, and never created cert.pem, a tunnel or a DNS route. The completed states are described from the official docs and the project's behavior; we did not trade a real authorization for a screenshot.
The previous step already had real effects: the authorization link usually appears after the local settings are saved and the add-on restarts. Save & Restart in Config writes to Supervisor options and then restarts the add-on; do not treat it as a harmless button that only opens the authorization page.

What “one-time” really means: treat Setup as a single flow you use only when you need that first local authorization, not as a permanent bookmark. Once it succeeds, Setup decides authorization is complete by detecting cert.pem, and stops showing that sign-in URL.
Authorization is not the same as working in public: seeing Certificate found only proves the local certificate exists. The tunnel still has to be created, and the edge connection, DNS and Home Assistant proxy settings still have to pass; do not skip the acceptance checks that follow.

Authorize safely in your own environment

  1. Confirm the mode, your fallback and the change window

    Finish Chapter 4 first: the Dashboard Mode should read local with no tunnel_token, and you should be able to sign in to Home Assistant from your home Wi-Fi using the local URL. If the tunnel's remote entrance is your only way in, stop here for now.

  2. Check the minimal settings you have already saved

    Confirm that the external hostname you plan to use, or any additional host you need, belongs to a Cloudflare zone you control, and that it overwrites no important existing DNS record. If you have not saved them yet, schedule a maintenance window before you use Save & Restart from Config; it writes to Supervisor options and then restarts the add-on.

  3. Wait in Setup for the link to appear

    After the restart, go back to Setup and wait for Authorize with Cloudflare to show Open Cloudflare authorization page. Do not assemble the URL yourself out of Logs, and do not press Copy link and then forward it over chat, email or a cloud clipboard.

  4. Open it directly on the same trusted device

    Choose Open Cloudflare authorization page and let the browser open it in a new tab. Check that the address bar really is an official Cloudflare domain, and sign in with your own password manager and multi-factor authentication. If it redirects to an unfamiliar domain or raises a certificate warning, close it at once.

  5. Check the account and the zone character by character

    Confirm you are signed in to the Cloudflare account you meant to use, and pick the zone that owns your planned hostname on the authorization page. If you manage home, company and client domains at the same time, do not go by a name that merely looks similar; if you cannot be sure, cancel, and never fall back on "just pick one and see".

  6. Pick the right domain and return to Setup

    Follow the Cloudflare screens to pick the domain that owns your planned hostname, and once authorization is done, do not capture the success page and do not keep the authorization URL. What this produces is an account certificate that can manage the account's tunnels; do not mistake it for something that applies to a single page. Go back to the Web GUI: the add-on should carry on by itself, Setup should change from waiting to Certificate found, and it then creates the tunnel, creates the DNS route and tries to connect.

  7. Let the automatic flow run; do not authorize twice

    Setup updates itself. Wait for Tunnel created and connected, then go to Logs for the first clear result. Do not reopen the link, authorize again, refresh the Cloudflare success page or restart repeatedly just because nothing turned green within a few seconds.

  8. Run the end-to-end and privacy acceptance checks

    Use the test table in this chapter to confirm the certificate, the tunnel, the edge connection, DNS and external HTTPS. At the same time, confirm you have left no authorization URL, zone list, account name, tunnel UUID, IP address or full Logs in a screenshot, a download or a shared clipboard.

How to get help safely: if the people you live with need technical help, the account owner signs in and presses the authorization in person, on a trusted device. Whoever is helping sees only a redacted screen or the error summary they need, and receives no link, password, MFA code or certificate.

Do not chase an all-green screenshot: the authorization page, the zone name, the account and the UUID can all expose your environment. A maintenance record only needs the date, the mode, the result and a redacted error code; do not keep sensitive success screens.

Protecting authorization data and handling a leak

  1. Verify the official domain and TLS

    Open the link only from Setup itself, and confirm the browser shows an official Cloudflare HTTPS domain with no certificate warning. Do not use a search ad, a shortened URL or a sign-in page a stranger sent you.

  2. Turn on MFA for the Cloudflare account

    Authorization reaches your DNS and tunnel management rights, so the account needs its own strong password and multi-factor authentication. MFA recovery codes are just as sensitive: keep them out of public notes and never send them to whoever is helping you.

  3. Narrow what is visible and what you share

    Do not photograph the authorization page, the zone list, the account menu, full Logs or the Setup URL. When you ask for help, say only that the link never appeared, or paste the first error, redacted and cut down to the smallest range.

  4. Protect the local data directory

    cert.pem and the tunnel credentials are used by the add-on; they do not need to be downloaded to your everyday computer, a shared NAS folder or a version-control repository. A Home Assistant backup also contains sensitive settings, so encrypt it and restrict access.

  5. Stop the flow the moment you suspect a leak

    If the URL, a session cookie, a token or a certificate has been posted anywhere, first stop the authorization and any change that goes public. Sign suspicious sessions out of Cloudflare and Home Assistant, review tunnel, DNS and account activity, then revoke or rotate the affected credentials the official way.

  6. Deleting a message does not replace revoking

    A chat message, an issue or a recording may already have been copied. Deleting the public content only reduces further exposure; it does not prove the secret went unread. Treat it as leaked, and work through the account and credential steps.

Never share, under any circumstances: the authorization URL, your Cloudflare password, an MFA code, cert.pem, a tunnel token, tunnel credentials, a session cookie or an unredacted log. Not even when the other side claims to be official support, and not over chat.

Which layer to stop at when it fails: Certificate found but no tunnel created means you look at the creation stage; Tunnel running but a 502 from outside means you look at DNS, ingress and the origin. Do not blame everything on authorization and run the whole thing again.

Common Cloudflare authorization pitfalls

  1. The authorization button never appears

    First confirm the Dashboard says local, that Setup is not in an unconfigured state, and that the add-on started with a complete configuration. Look for the first prepare error in Logs; do not generate a URL yourself, clear the certificate or press Restart over and over.

  2. The link opens expired, invalid or on an error page

    Close the old page, and do not paste the URL onto another device to test it. Go back to Setup to see whether the flow is still waiting, and check the host clock, DNS and outbound connectivity. If a new link is needed, work out the cause from Logs first, then do a single restart inside a maintenance window.

  3. You sign in but your planned zone is not there

    The usual causes are signing in to the wrong account, a domain that does not use that account's Cloudflare DNS, or an identity without enough rights. Cancel the authorization and check on your Cloudflare account page; do not pick a zone that looks similar but is not the one you need this time.

  4. You authorized, but Setup is still waiting

    Wait for the page to update itself once more, then check Logs for a successful certificate write or a permission error. Do not resubmit the success page; if cert.pem was never created, keep the first error, redacted, for troubleshooting.

  5. Certificate found, but tunnel creation fails

    Authorization is already done; there is no need to redo it first. Check whether the tunnel name is unique within the same Cloudflare account, and look at API connectivity and permission errors; fix only what you can clearly identify, then retry once.

  6. The tunnel is created but the hostname does not work

    Move on to the Dashboard, Cloudflare DNS, the Home Assistant HTTP proxy and the origin layer. Confirm the edge connections, that DNS points only at the intended tunnel, and that trusted_proxies follows the official docs; do not authorize again.

  7. You shared the authorization URL or a screen by accident

    Stop the flow at once. Delete the public content, but do not assume that finishes the job: sign suspicious sessions out, review Cloudflare account, tunnel and DNS activity, revoke or rotate any credential that may be affected the official way, and replace any token that leaked.

Authorization FAQ

Does a remotely managed tunnel need this authorization link too?
No. Remotely managed mode uses the tunnel_token the Cloudflare dashboard gives you; Setup shows a token-mode notice, and the locally managed authorization, certificate and four-step flow do not apply.
Can I copy the link to my phone and finish it there?
We do not recommend sending it over chat, email or a cross-device clipboard. The safest approach is to open it directly on the same trusted device that is showing Setup; if that device is not trusted, stop rather than carry a sensitive URL around.
Does authorization produce a website HTTPS certificate?
No. cert.pem is the account certificate cloudflared uses for a locally managed tunnel, not the website TLS certificate a browser sees; protect it as a secret all the same.
Once I see Certificate found, can I sign in from outside?
Not necessarily. Tunnel creation, the DNS route, the edge connection, the Home Assistant proxy settings and an external HTTPS test all still have to be done; work through the acceptance table in this chapter layer by layer.
I picked the wrong zone while authorizing. Can I carry on and change it later?
Do not go on to create routes. Stop the flow, check your Cloudflare account and anything it has already created, then revoke the wrong authorization or certificate the official way; only once you have confirmed things are clean, redo it with the right zone.
Why does this guide not include a screenshot of a successful authorization?
Because the authorization page shows the account, the zone and sensitive flow data, and reaching the completed state takes a real change. We deliberately do not authorize in the capture environment, and describe it in words and from official sources instead.
