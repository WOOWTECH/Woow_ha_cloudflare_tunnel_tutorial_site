Why your Home Assistant needs a tunnel

Home Assistant is safest sitting in your own house, but as soon as you want to check on the house or control an appliance from outside, you run into the same three old problems:

  • You have to open a port on the router: exposing one of your home ports to the whole world is like pinning a notice to your front door that says "there is a service here". DDoS, brute-force attempts and scanners then run day and night.
  • You need a static IP or DDNS: either you pay your ISP extra, or the connection drops when the IP changes overnight. DDNS takes time to take effect, and being locked out of the app is painful.
  • You need an SSL certificate: unencrypted HTTP is your password written on a postcard, and running Let's Encrypt yourself means maintaining the renewals.

Cloudflare Tunnel settles all three at once: cloudflared dials out from your house to the Cloudflare edge and holds a long-lived connection open, and anything coming in has to pass through that Cloudflare layer — your home IP and ports are completely invisible from outside.

Concept: traditional port forwarding leaves your front door open a crack and hopes the bad guys do not find it. A tunnel has no door at all, only one encrypted private line that you dialed out yourself. The security model is a different thing entirely.

What Cloudflare Tunnel is

It is Cloudflare's free tunnel service. A program called cloudflared runs on the Home Assistant in your house and connects out to the Cloudflare edge. After that, anyone who visits your domain reaches Cloudflare first, and Cloudflare carries the request home over that tunnel.

The Home Assistant Cloudflared Web GUI add-on from WoowTech wraps the whole flow in a graphical interface: add the repository, press install, run the Setup wizard, finish Cloudflare authorization, and in 5 minutes you have an external entrance at https://ha.your-domain.example without reading a line of YAML.

Tip: the Cloudflare account is free, DNS is free, the tunnel is free and the SSL certificate is free. You just need a domain of your own (roughly USD 10–20 a year); everything else starts at zero.

What Zero Trust is, and why a home needs it

"Zero Trust" is not a marketing word, it is a security model: trust nobody by default, and verify identity on every connection. The traditional approach is "once you are through the front door you are one of us", so the moment a port is found or a password is guessed it is over. Zero Trust says "even once you are inside, every room still needs a badge".

Cloudflare Access puts a doorkeeper in front of Home Assistant: when anyone visits your URL, Cloudflare first checks whether that identity is on the allowlist, and only then passes the request to the Home Assistant in your house. The Home Assistant sign-in page is not visible from outside at all.

The default policies WoowTech sets up for you

  • Your own account: your Gmail or Google Workspace address, allowed on every path, with a 24-hour session.
  • Family accounts: an allowlist of your family's Gmail addresses, allowed on /lovelace/* and blocked on /config/*.
  • Temporary guests: Email OTP with a 6-hour session, good for letting a contractor check a status page.
  • Country restriction: allow only your own country's IP ranges, so from anywhere else even the Access sign-in page is invisible.
Warning: Home Assistant advises against exposing the admin interface directly. Even with a strong password and 2FA, brute-force attempts, CVEs and browser-extension holes are all live attack surface. Cloudflare Access keeps Home Assistant behind the door, so an attacker never gets the chance to knock — that is what Zero Trust means for a home.

FAQ

Can Cloudflare see the data from my Home Assistant?
Cloudflare relays encrypted traffic. It can see metadata (source IP, time, hostname, path) but not the contents of the HTTPS body. This is the same as it is for any website you put on Cloudflare. If the metadata matters to you too, Option B can add Cloudflare Workers for a second layer of end-to-end encryption.
Can I use it without a domain of my own?
Cloudflare Tunnel needs a domain whose DNS is hosted by Cloudflare. Under Option A we can help you register one (.com / .net / .app all work, roughly USD 10–35 a year) and move it onto Cloudflare. Under Option B you can use a subdomain such as yourhome.woowtech.cloud instead, so there is nothing to buy.
Does it work on Home Assistant OS, Supervised and Container?
On HAOS and Supervised you just run our Cloudflared Web GUI add-on. On Container or Core you can run cloudflared separately with docker-compose, and Option A covers that too. ARM, x86 and Raspberry Pi 3B or newer are all compatible.
What happens if the tunnel drops?
The Cloudflared add-on has a built-in watchdog that reconnects on its own, so a short outage recovers by itself. Option B adds 7×24 monitoring and a push notification when the tunnel drops, so once the notification reaches your phone you can restart the Home Assistant host as soon as you are home. Even during a long outage you can still reach Home Assistant on its local IP from the local network.
